## 2. What the audit measures

For each venue, a stratified sample (target ≥ 300 open and ≥ 300 recently resolved markets; strata: sports by sport, esports, short-term crypto, other crypto, politics, culture/other, from tags when
present, else title/slug keywords: `categorize`, a heuristic whose error rate is unmeasured):

1. **Inventory** of every time-like field on market and event objects (nested objects flattened to dotted paths; `events[].x` = the first event): name, what the *name* suggests (`classifyFieldName`), format (date-only, UTC datetime, datetime with offset, lenient `YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM:SS+00`, offset-less, epoch), precision, offset, presence by stratum.
2. **Meaning, by evidence** (never assumed): for resolved markets, each candidate field against every resolution-like field the venue provides (`closedTime`-style) and against **our own** observed resolution time (`--with-db`: the 150 most recently resolved tokens in `token_resolutions`, their markets fetched from Gamma, select-only reads); ordering of every pair of candidate fields; start-like fields against creation-like fields (a start that equals the creation time is a listing time).
3. **Placeholders**: date-only values; exact `00:00:00`, `12:00:00`, `23:59:59` UTC (and wall-clock local midnight / end of day when an offset is present); the time-of-day histogram (a field with one dominant time of day is a hidden placeholder); instants repeated across many unrelated markets (a group = one event or title family; markets of one event legitimately share a time).
4. **Disagreements**: between fields of one market (the relation tables) and, for events that exist on both venues (matched with `matchSignalToVenue`, confidence EXACT or PROBABLE only), between the venues' recommended start fields: the spread is the input to D51 (`TIMESTAMP_TOLERANCE`, proposed 15 min).
5. **Recommendation per stratum, venue and slot** by explicit, printed rules (`RECOMMEND_RULES`, `src/lib/phase4/audit.ts`). A field is recommended only if all hold; otherwise "unreliable: reject" (enough data, a rule failed) or "insufficient data":

| Rule (slot 1 = `event_start_time`, slot 2 = `market_close_time`) | Threshold (proposed; the owner may change it) |
|---|---|
| present on enough markets of the stratum | ≥ 30 |
| usable share: a datetime with time of day and zone, not a placeholder shape, over **all** markets of the stratum | ≥ 90 % |
| placeholder share of the values present | ≤ 10 % |
| no single UTC time of day on more than this share of the values | ≤ 50 % |
| slot 1: start ≤ the earliest resolution/closure reference (venue's or ours) in resolved markets (≥ 30 such markets, else *insufficient data*: meaning not established) | ≥ 99 % |
| slot 1, event-type strata (sports, esports, short-term crypto): median start→resolution | ≤ 24 h |
| slot 1: not coinciding (±60 min) with a creation-like field | ≤ 50 % of markets |
| slot 1: start ≤ a close-like field **that itself carries a real time of day** (placeholder closes are skipped) + 15 min | ≥ 95 % |
| slot 2 | the value rules only; **always flagged for human review**: a deadline's relation to resolution cannot be asserted without knowing what the venue means by it |

Why these rules exist is visible in the tests: running the first version on the synthetic world exposed that (a) a midnight-placeholder close precedes almost every kick-off, so comparing a start with it rejects a good field; and (b) a listing time passes every ordering rule unless it is compared with the creation time. Both were fixed and are pinned by tests.

Output files (`--out-dir`, default `docs/phase4/data`): `s1a_polymarket_intl.json`, `s1a_polymarket_us.json` (inventory, per-stratum evidence, recommendations), `s1a_summary.json` (also read by the S1b probe), `S1a_RESULTS.md` (generated tables); sanitised raw samples to `tests/fixtures/phase4/s1a_<venue>_{open,resolved}.json` (first ≤ 40 markets each, ≤ 380 kB, long strings truncated, images/descriptions removed).

## 5. How to run

```
npm run phase4:ts-audit -- --with-db        # needs NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_URL and SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY (select only); omit --with-db for network only
# options: --out-dir docs/phase4/data  --fixtures-dir tests/fixtures/phase4 | --no-fixtures  --sample-open 300 --sample-resolved 300 --fetch-factor 3

```
≈ 2 requests/second: about 10 listing pages per venue plus up to 150 single-market reads with `--with-db` (≈ 3 minutes). Prints ≤ 60 lines (Railway-safe); exit 0 even when a venue is unreachable. Commit `docs/phase4/data/s1a_*.json` and the new fixtures; the S1b probe reads `s1a_summary.json`.

## 8. Step 4.0b: event-level reliability, Eastern-time placeholders, `gameStartTime`, venue agreement

Why: the first production run (3 Oct 2026, reported by the owner; its result files are not in this repository) showed that in most strata one clock time held most values (04:59:00, 03:59:00, 04:00:00 UTC …), and raised a method concern: the rules counted **markets**, but markets of one event share their times. That concern was right: with the market as the unit, a sample dominated by a few events looked like "one clock time holds more than half" even for a genuine start time. Nothing in this section was measured by the step that wrote it (no venue access); it is code, fixture tests and method.

### 8.1 Every rule is evaluated per distinct event (`recommendSlots`, `collapseToEvents`)

- The event of a market is the venue's event identifier (`events[].id`/`slug` on the international venue, `event.id`/`eventSlug` on the US venue), else a key from the **normalised title and the end date** (`eventFallbackKey`); never the market's own slug or id.
- **One representative market per event** (the first in input order) carries the event into every rule: presence, usable share, placeholder share, dominant clock time, ordering against resolution, coincidence with a creation time, the start→resolution gap, the start-before-close comparison. Limitation: a field that differs between the markets of one event is not flagged; the first market's value is used.
- The minimum is now **30 distinct events** (was 30 markets), for presence and for the resolved events used as ordering evidence. The thresholds themselves are **unchanged** (owner decision D68; `tests/phase4-events.test.ts` pins them).
- Test on a hand-built world (`tests/helpers/phase4EventWorld.ts`): 6 events × 20 markets with 100 of 120 markets sharing one clock time is **INSUFFICIENT_DATA (6 < 30 events)**, not a placeholder; 60 distinct events all at 04:59 UTC is rejected; 60 events with varied kick-offs is recommended; 29 events × 10 markets stays insufficient; 30 events × 1 market is enough.
- **Every verdict row now carries** `events`, `markets` (in the stratum), `etPlaceholderShare`, `decidedBy` (the first failing rule: `minEvents`, `usableShare`, `placeholderShare`, `topClock`, `orderedShare`, `creationCoincidence`, `eventTypeGap`, `startBeforeClose`, `evidenceGap`, or `allPassed`) and `failedRules`. The printed summary shows them; the results page has the full table. `S1a_RESULTS.md` also lists markets and **distinct events per stratum**.
- Sampling: within each stratum the quota is spent on the first market of every distinct event before any second market of an event (`stratifiedSample`), so a fixed sample size reaches as many events as the listing holds.
- If you think a threshold is wrong: this step could not assess that without data. One observation from the method, not a recommendation: at event level the 50 % dominant-clock rule is a statement about distinct events, which is what it was meant to be; whether 50 % is the right level can only be read from event-level counts in a real run.

### 8.2 Eastern-time date placeholders (`easternPlaceholder`, `impliedEasternDate`, `ET_MIDNIGHT` / `ET_END_OF_DAY`)

A value whose **America/New_York wall clock** is exactly `00:00:00` (`ET_MIDNIGHT`: 04:00Z in summer, 05:00Z in winter) or `23:59:00`/`23:59:59` (`ET_END_OF_DAY`: 03:59Z / 04:59Z of the next UTC day) is a date written as a time. The offset comes from the IANA zone (Node's `Intl`), so the daylight-saving change is handled by the rules of the zone, not by a fixed offset: tests cover both offsets, the autumn change (1 Nov 2026) and the spring change (8 Mar 2026), and the wrong-offset look-alikes (04:00Z in winter is 23:00 the evening before: not a placeholder).

- **Implied calendar date**: the Eastern date of the instant (`ET_MIDNIGHT`), the date that is ending (`ET_END_OF_DAY`: 03:59Z on the 6th implies the 5th), a date-only value as written, `null` for a genuine time of day. It is a date, never a time.
- `placeholderKind` now returns the ET kinds (after the UTC and wall-clock kinds), so these values count as placeholders in the usable and placeholder shares; the inventory lists them per field, and every verdict row shows `etPlaceholderShare`.
- **Date-level alternative rows** (`dateLevelRows`, section "Date-level alternative" of the results page): per stratum, the close-like or start-like field whose values most often imply an Eastern date, how many distinct events that covers, split by kind (ET midnight / ET end of day / date-only), whether the implied date is not after the Eastern date of the resolution (and the median days before it). **Shown, never recommended**: `recommended` is the literal `false`, the label is `ALTERNATIVE_DATE_LEVEL`, and each row says the in-play check cannot be evaluated at date level (a date cannot say whether the event has started or how long before it an order would fill). The verdict rules are untouched.

### 8.3 `gameStartTime` deep dive (`gameStartDeepDive`, per venue, in `s1a_<venue>.json` under `gameStart`)

Presence by sport and by market type (markets and distinct events; the market-type field is looked for in the market objects: `sportsMarketType(s)`, `marketType`, `market_type`); which sports reach **≥ 100 markets from ≥ 30 distinct events** (both required; a sport that cannot is reported as not reaching it); agreement with the event-level `startTime` (one pair per event, equal within a minute); the relation to every creation-like field; **resolution minus game start per sport** (positive share, share within 12 h and 24 h, p10/p50/p90 in hours: it should be positive and bounded by a game's length); the time of day **per distinct event**, in UTC and in Eastern.

**US filters.** The brief names `sportsMarketTypes` (MONEYLINE, SPREAD, TOTAL, PROP) and `categories`; their query syntax and category values are **not verified here**. `--us-targeted default` sends one query per major sport (football, basketball, baseball, hockey, soccer, tennis, combat) built from those names (a guess); `--us-targeted "sport=query|sport=query"` replaces them with queries written from `docs.polymarket.us`. Each targeted query is paged until it has ≥ 100 markets from ≥ 30 distinct events or the listing ends (≤ 2 requests/second); the markets are added to the US sample whole; the results page lists, per sport, markets, events, whether the quota was **reached** and why it stopped; a wrong query shows as an error or an empty page and the stratum is reported as unable to reach it.

### 8.4 The same event on both venues (`matchEventsAcrossVenues`)

Events (not markets) are matched by **participant names** parsed from "A vs B" titles (either home/away order, the part before a colon or bracket only, similarity ≥ 0.8 on both sides) and by **Eastern date** (within one day). A tie between two equally good candidates is counted as ambiguous and skipped, never picked. For matched events with a start time on both venues (`gameStartTime`, else an event-level `startTime`, else `eventStartTime`; never `startDate`, which is a listing time) the spread of the start-time differences is reported overall and by stratum (|diff| p50/p90/p95/p99/max, share within 15 and 60 minutes): the input to D51. Names that differ in spelling ("Man City" / "Manchester City" scores 0.33) are **not** matched without an alias list; the counts of head-to-head events per venue and of matched and ambiguous events show how much that costs.

## 9. Step 4.0c: every candidate judged per slot, Kalshi, the sports API, `live` / `ended`, schedule sources

Nothing in this section was measured by the step that wrote it (no venue was reachable; see README "Status of the measurements"). The tests run every path on fixtures with hand-derived answers.

### 9.1 The slot-1 misreading of 4.0b, its cause and the fix
The 4.0b verdict table printed one field per stratum and slot: the candidate with the **fewest definitive failures**. `startDate` (rejected: a listing time) was printed for American football, basketball, hockey, combat and other sports, and `gameStartTime`, which shows varied, plausible kick-off times per distinct event, was never shown. The cause is in the evidence, not the thresholds: the targeted US queries fetched **open** events only, so `gameStartTime` had no resolved events to be ordered against the resolution times (rule `orderedShare` needs ≥ 30 resolved events with a reference time), and a candidate with *no evidence* sorts after one with a *failed* rule. Two changes, neither touching a threshold (D68):
1. **Every candidate is judged and reported.** Each `SlotVerdict` now carries `candidates[]` (all START_LIKE fields, plus neutrally named time fields such as a Kalshi `event.strike_date`, for slot 1; all CLOSE_LIKE fields for slot 2), each with its own verdict (`RECOMMEND` / `UNRELIABLE_REJECT` / `INSUFFICIENT_DATA`), the rule that decided it, every rule it failed, presence, usable share and its evidence; and `bestField`, the best **passing** candidate or null (the answer to "which field fills the slot"). `field` keeps the older single-field pick so existing consumers still read `s1a_summary.json`. A candidate with no ordering evidence is `INSUFFICIENT_DATA (evidenceGap)`, never "unusable".
2. **Ended events are fetched.** The sports API fetch (below) takes the ended side of every sport, and the category-slug fallback queries include `closed=true`, so the ordering evidence exists.

If a rule looks wrong the report says so with numbers; the thresholds are unchanged (`RECOMMEND_RULES`, pinned by a test).

### 9.2 `gameStartTime` on single-game markets versus FUTURES markets
`gameStartTime` is present on futures markets too. Per sport and class (single-game types: MONEYLINE, SPREAD, TOTAL, PROP and any other non-futures type; futures; unclassified) the audit reports: events and presence, distinct times of day and the top one, `gameStartTime − creation` in hours (p10/p50/p90), the share within an hour of a creation-like field, and `resolution − gameStartTime` (n, p10/p50/p90, share within 24 h). The verdict per sport (`futuresVerdicts`) is a stated rule: **INSUFFICIENT_DATA** below 30 events with the field in either class; **SINGLE_GAME_ONLY** when the single-game median resolution−start is within 24 h and the futures class does not look like a start (median above 24 h, or more than half within an hour of creation); **NO_DIFFERENCE_OBSERVED** otherwise. It is a report, not a policy: whether `gameStartTime` may be used for single-game market types only is the owner's call (D81).

### 9.3 Kalshi as a third venue (`--kalshi`, `--kalshi-max 40000`, `--kalshi-base URL`)
`src/lib/phase4/venue-kalshi.ts`; the API as recorded is in [`KALSHI_API.md`](KALSHI_API.md) (**not verified**). The listing is `GET /events?status=…&with_nested_markets=true` for open, closed and settled markets, exploded into markets that keep their event, **slimmed on arrival**, paged by cursor, capped (default 40,000 markets: 50 % open, 10 % closed, 40 % settled). The audit records how many open, closed and settled markets exist by Kalshi category (`s1a_kalshi.json` → `listing`, and a table in `S1a_RESULTS.md`), the time-field inventory, and the per-candidate verdicts. Field roles: `close_time` CLOSE_LIKE; `open_time` CREATION_LIKE; `expected_expiration_time`, `latest_expiration_time` and the deprecated `expiration_time` RESOLUTION_LIKE (the last on Kalshi only); `event.strike_date` neutral, therefore judged as a slot-1 candidate by the same rules. No Kalshi market field is START_LIKE by name.

### 9.4 The US sports API, per-sport samples, open and ended (`--us-targeted default`)
`--us-targeted default` now means: discover the sports and leagues (`GET /v2/sports`, `GET /v2/leagues`), then for each sport fetch its open events (`active=true`) and ended events (`closed=true`) by sport slug (`/v2/sports/{slug}/events`), falling back to the sport's league slugs (`/v2/leagues/{slug}/events`), each side until ≥ 100 markets from ≥ 30 distinct events (D76) or the listing ends, within a request budget (`--us-sports-max-requests`, default 400; reaching it says so). If discovery returns no markets, the corrected category-slug queries stand in (`categories=sports` with `sportsMarketTypes` MONEYLINE, SPREAD, TOTAL, PROP, open and ended; `categories=crypto`). The 4.0b guess `categories=football` (HTTP 400) is gone (D77); `--us-targeted "name=query|name=query"` still overrides, and a wrong query is reported in the targeted table. The US origin is paced at ≥ 1.1 s per request (its documented public limit is 60 per minute, **not verified**), Gamma and Kalshi at the brief's 2 per second.

### 9.5 The `live` / `ended` indicator: measured, not adopted
`inPlayReliability` (`s1a_inplay_us.json`, a section of `S1a_RESULTS.md`) takes a snapshot of the sampled events plus the events the venue itself flags `live` and `ended` (`GET /v1/events?live=true`, `?ended=true`; capped) and reports: events by time to the scheduled start with their live share and ended count; **live while the start is more than 5 minutes away** (false positive for "in play"), how many and how early; for live events that had started, minutes since the start (p10/p50/p90/max), which brackets when the flag flips; **started 5–180 minutes ago and neither live nor ended** (false negatives: the flag is missing, or the event finished unflagged); `ended` against the markets' resolved status (flagged events whose markets are all resolved, flagged with an open market, resolved events not flagged), and the contradictions (live and ended at once, live with everything resolved). It is a **cross-sectional snapshot**: it brackets the flip time but does not time it; timing it needs repeated snapshots across a game's start, which a later run can add. Evidence for or against plan §3.4's in-play check; nothing is adopted (D85).

### 9.6 Schedule sources
`scheduleCompare` (US: the event-level start from the sports endpoint against the market's `gameStartTime`; Kalshi: a milestone's `start_date` against the event's `strike_date`) reports per sport or category: how many carry a time of day (not a placeholder shape), how many pairs have both, how many agree within a minute and within 15 minutes, |difference| p50/p90/max, and the placeholder count. `/milestones` is unconfirmed; its absence is a note.
