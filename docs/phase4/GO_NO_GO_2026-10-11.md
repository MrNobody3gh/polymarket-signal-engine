## All of these must be true for a GO (a tiny, capped, manual pilot, never automation)

1. **G1 eligibility.** A South African lawyer or accountant has advised in writing that the owner may use international Polymarket and how the gains are taxed, **and** Polymarket's own restricted-jurisdictions page, checked on the decision day, does not list South Africa.
2. **Fee page re-checked.** The official fee page was read on the decision day and matches the measured 10 % rate, or the difference is understood and costed.
3. **Money the owner can lose entirely.** A total pilot budget, set by the owner in writing, small enough that losing all of it changes nothing. Suggested ceiling: $250 total, $10 per order, one order at a time.
4. **Expectation set honestly.** The owner accepts in writing that the pilot is a test of execution and cost, **not** evidence of edge, and that a few dozen trades cannot show profit (about 1,190 settled trades are needed for a ±5-point interval).
5. **Manual execution only.** Each order is placed by the owner from an alert. No key is given to any bot, no Grok authority, no automation. Phase 4.2 to 4.5 (gates, executor, reconciliation) are not built, and that rule stands.
6. **Pilot stop rule fixed in advance.** Stop at −$40 cumulative, or after 40 orders, or on any order that fills at a price more than 5 points worse than the alert, whichever comes first. Nothing is added to the pilot after a loss.
7. **Eligible flow only.** Only alerts that already satisfy policy V1 are traded. No discretionary trades, no other markets.

## Automatic NO-GO (any one is enough)

- The shadow recorder is down or the report cannot be produced.

## The cost hurdle (for the record)

A follower pays about 3.6 % of stake in fees plus about 2 points (≈ 4 % of stake) in slippage, roughly 6 to 8 % per trade at $25. To profit, the wallets' picks must beat that by enough to be seen above a per-trade standard deviation of about 88 points. Cost reduction (resting orders save roughly the 3.6 % fee but fill only about a third of the time, and the fills are the ones where the price moved against the wallet) is a separate workstream from selection value.

## Tax questions answered in writing, 8 Oct 2026 (second letter from the same firm)

- **Capital or revenue for a 40-order pilot:** treat as **revenue** unless the facts clearly support capital. Forty orders in a short window make the capital argument weak. Keep the workings either way.
- **Losses:** capital losses offset only capital gains. Revenue losses can reduce other income, **but** section 20A ring-fencing can lock losses to the trade if SARS treats the pilot as betting or the trade stays loss-making. **Do not assume a current-year offset.**
- **Records:** a rand schedule per order, kept five years (template in `PILOT_LOG_TEMPLATE.csv`, routine in `PILOT_RUNBOOK.md`).
- **Effect on the decision.** For budgeting, assume pilot **losses are not deductible** and any **gain is taxed at the marginal rate (up to 45 %)**. The pilot's after-tax result is therefore worse than its pre-tax result in the good case and no better in the bad case. This is one more reason the pilot is sized as money the owner can lose entirely (item 3), and it makes the pilot a cost-of-learning decision, not an income plan.
- **Item 1 (G1) is now met in full**: written advice on access and on tax, and the restricted page checked on 8 Oct (re-read on the decision day). Items 2 to 7 remain open.

## Item 2 (fee page) checked 9 Oct 2026 — NOT matched
Polymarket's official fee page: taker fee = shares × feeRate × p × (1 − p); feeRate Crypto 0.07, Sports 0.05, Culture/Economics/Weather/Other 0.05, Finance/Politics/Mentions/Tech 0.04, Geopolitics 0; makers never pay; maker rebates 15–25 %; a taker rebate programme exists (rates not given).
Measured by the recorder: the venue's fee-rate endpoint and the markets table both say **0.10** in every stratum (4,773 pairs). The two measurements agree with each other and disagree with the page by about a factor of two (0.10 vs 0.04 to 0.07).
What it means: fee ÷ stake = feeRate × (1 − p). The measured mean fee of 3.6 % of stake would be about 1.8 % at 0.05. Either the page is out of date or categorised differently from the endpoint, or the endpoint's figure is not the rate charged. **Unresolved.** The cost estimates in this memo (6 to 8 % per trade) may be about 1.8 points too high, or right. The first $10 pilot fill settles it (compare the fee actually charged with 0.10 and 0.05). Until then, treat the higher figure as the planning number.
Item 2 therefore stays **open**, but its resolution is the pilot's first order, not more research.
